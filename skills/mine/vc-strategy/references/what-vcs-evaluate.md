# What VCs Evaluate — the decision system the strategy must pass

Use this as a diagnostic rubric for the requested fundability question. The twelve criteria and numeric examples are not prerequisites for drafting another artifact.

The fundraise is designed against how VCs actually decide. Sources dated; conflicts shown, not averaged.

## Fund-returner arithmetic (the objective function)

A VC scores P(fund-returning outcome) × ownership, not company quality:

- Portfolio shape: of 10 investments, 1–2 home runs, 3–4 modest, 4–5 zeros (NFX). Angel bar: 20–50× in 5–7 years.
- Worked: $20M in → $100M exit returns capital, zero carry. $20M → $1B exit at 20% held → $200M, of which 20% carry = $40M — and only if the whole fund clears (NFX Non-Obvious Guide).
- Exit floor scales with fund size: "a $5–10M exit won't move the needle of a $500M fund" (Ed Kang).
- The question being asked is never "can this be a $5–6M business" — it's "can this be a **$100M-revenue** business" (NFX), which is why TAM must clear ~$1B bottom-up.

**Check-size math (compute before pitching any fund):** check ≈ **2% of fund size**, for **10–20% ownership** (seed funds threshold ~10–20%, Series A leads target ~20%). A $150M fund is comfortable putting $3M into a $20M post; ask it for $15M and it stalls. `implied_post ≈ target_check / 0.15`. "So many times a deal could have happened if the founders had just asked for less" (Jason Lemkin). If the ask doesn't land in a fund's band, change the fund or the number.

## The six criteria and their stage weights

Founders/FMF · unique insight · market · traction · defensibility · ask coherence. Weight shifts (2025–26 evidence):

| Criterion | Pre-seed | Seed | Series A |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder / FMF | Dominant | Dominant — execution over pedigree (CRV 2026) | High, but "the center of gravity moves from *you* to the machine you built" (Graham & Walker 2025) |
| Unique insight | Dominant | High | Must be evidenced by repeatable results |
| Market | Credible bottom-up, $1B+ | $1B+; ~20% share is the realistic capture ceiling (CRV) | Segment proof + expansion map |
| Traction | LOIs, pilots, prepays | Revenue is the 2026 baseline: $500K–$1.5M ARR for a *strong* B2B seed (CRV) | $2–3M ARR — "$3M is the new $1M" (G&W) |
| Defensibility | Argued from insight | Early moat mechanics | **Existential** — "why not anyone else with the same tools" |
| Process discipline | Low | Medium | High — the process itself is evaluated |

2026 reframe for technical founders: product is assumed cheap to build, so the question moved from "what have you built?" to "how well is it working, and **does the wedge repeat** outside the founder's hands?" (Graham & Walker). Shipping velocity is table stakes; wedge repeatability is the evidence.

## The screening gates (each is a sufficient no)

Ed Kang's sequence: (1) does it make sense in 1–2 minutes of deck — incoherent = instant pass; (2) thesis fit (geography/stage/sector — "usually has nothing to do with you"); (3) market size, bottom-up; (4) why now — and why prior attempts failed; (5) what's different — killer test: *if a $1B incumbent could throw $10–30M at this and solve it your way, I'm out*; (6) why you — domain expertise, chemistry, tech in-house. Reframe worth designing for: "You're not trying to get the investor to make an investment decision. You're helping them make a **sorting** decision" — optimize the top of every artifact for fast, accurate sorting.

## The partnership as a decision system

The deal is argued in a room the founder is not in:

- **Odds ladder:** after a first 1:1 ≈ **5%** chance of investment; reaching the partner meeting lifts it to a **25–60%** offer rate at quoted firms (First Round, 2024) — though a funnel-wide read says ~1 in 6 at PM stage (INSEAD via Qubit, 2026). Both agree the PM is the biggest odds jump.
- **The champion gets ~30 seconds** at the Monday partner meeting to justify bringing the company in (NFX). Everything in the strategy must survive 30-second retelling by someone else.
- **Deal memos** are written even on passes; 3–30 pages at seed; ten sections: overview · team · vitamin-or-painkiller · market size *and direction* · why now · monetization · traction · **unfair advantage** · **risks (what takes this to zero)** · decision. Sequoia's gate: the whole thesis must compress to **under one page**.
- **IC variance is real:** some seed partnerships approve nearly everything that reaches IC (sponsor conviction rules); Series A committees actually kill deals. Ask, don't assume: "what is the role of the partner meeting in your process? what % of companies that reach it get a term sheet?"

**Founder counter-move — write your own memo.** All ten sections including risks. "In the second sentence she calls out her biggest risk… instead of making me nervous it's making me trust her more — self-awareness is a real moat" (Gabriel Jarrosson). "Founders who write memos outperform founders who just pitch."

## Herd dynamics (the FOMO mechanism, from primary source)

Paul Graham: "Investors are pinched between two kinds of fear: fear of investing in startups that fizzle, and fear of missing out on startups that take off… they all wait as long as they can, then when some act the rest have to." Four mechanisms, each a founder lever (*Investor Herd Dynamics*, 2013):

1. Raising money genuinely de-risks the company — it's worth more after the wire.
2. Success makes founders visibly confident, and investors read people ("the average founder's inability to remain poker-faced works to your advantage").
3. Most investors can't judge startups — "the mediocre ones might as well be flipping coins" — so they proxy on other investors' interest.
4. Live interest imposes a deadline on everyone else.

Corollaries: the **first substantial commitment is half the total difficulty** ($50K from a known angel starts the cascade); stampedes are *uncorrelated* with success; the most common investor question is "**who else is investing?**"; and never name the other firms in play (VCs — angels are sometimes fine).

## Fundability self-assessment (run before any artifact is built)

1. Name the exit that returns the target fund — is there a credible argued path there?
2. Is the ask ≈2% of the target fund's size, implying 10–20% ownership at the target valuation?
3. Bottom-up path to $100M revenue stated in arithmetic, not top-down TAM?
4. Core capability in-house — no "raising to hire the CTO" (instant rejection: CRV; Lemkin: "I won't invest without an S-tier CTO").
5. The one-sentence contrarian insight: what does the team know that everyone else disagrees with?
6. "Why not anyone else with the same tools?" — the 2026 defensibility bar.
7. Does the wedge repeat outside the founders' hands? Has a non-founder closed?
8. Next 10 customers named by title, company size, specific pain ("growth marketing managers at Series A SaaS companies with $2–10M ARR struggling with attribution" — not "SMBs").
9. **3–4 months of strong growth, not one** — "1 month isn't a trend" (Lemkin); VCs overlook a flat period followed by 3–4 strong months.
10. A smart generalist can restate the pitch accurately (grandmother test).
11. The founder-side memo exists, risks section included, compressible to one page.
12. Cap table: <20 holders, founders ≥70% collectively post-seed, every SAFE modeled at cap.

Verdict rules: any fail on 1–4 → **fix first** or **different capital**; fails on 5–8 → the narrative/evidence work of Steps 3–4; fails on 9–12 → timing and hygiene fixes before launch.

## Champion-arming kit (built at strategy time, used all through the raise)

- A one-page memo in the target firm's own vocabulary — readable aloud in 30 seconds.
- Three sentences the champion can repeat verbatim: the insight, the proof, the why-now.
- A pre-answered risks list — every memo will have one; the strategy decides whether it's the founder's version or the skeptic's.
- An FAQ for technical depth so the memo gets the architecture right and the meeting stays on story.
- Named customer references ready to hand over — supplied references collapse diligence from weeks to ~24h.
- Per-investor emphasis notes: what *this* firm knows how to judge (network effects at NFX-shaped funds, bottom-up+top-down at Homebrew-shaped, infra metrics at AI-infra funds). Currier ran 60 deck variants for 30 investors.

## Structural facts worth designing around

- Seed VCs see ~1,000 companies/year, invest in 4–5; Series A partners do 1–2 deals/year each; overall ~1–2% of opportunities reach a term sheet.
- VC behaviour is fund-structure-driven: "VCs don't invest $x million because that's the amount you need, but because that's the amount the structure of their business requires them to invest" (PG, 2005). Oversized checks, slow decisions, board control — structural, not personal.
- Missionary beats mercenary at the thesis level: funds explicitly back founders who "truly relate to the problem" for a 7–10-year journey; devtools pattern that repeatedly gets funded: **bottom-up adoption + top-down sales** (Homebrew).
- "Lines, not dots" (Suster): investors price trajectory across interactions, not a snapshot — which is why vc-outreach's update cadence starts before the raise.
- Treat every investor as a no until an unequivocal yes with no contingencies, in writing (PG: "hear no till you hear yes").
