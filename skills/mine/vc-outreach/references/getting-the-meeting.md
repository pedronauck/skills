# Getting the Meeting — warm machinery, cold spec, follow-up rules

Use the requested channel and existing authorization. Length, timing, batch-size, warm-up, and cadence recipes below are experiments, not mandatory stages. Prepare messages; do not send or contact people without explicit authorization.

Four access channels (OpenVC's Fundraising Wheel): warm intros, cold outreach, inbound, existing network. Ranked paths to a first meeting (NFX): warm intro from someone the partner trusts → competitive momentum → KPIs/proof → the X factor. An intro buys ~5 minutes of attention; a cold email buys ~2 seconds.

## Warm-intro machinery

**The forwardable blurb** (Hustle Fund's anatomy — the single most important asset once the list is built):
1. CTA to the referrer at the very top ("feel free to forward this along") — nothing more.
2. One line: what the company does.
3. **2–3 bullets** of the most compelling facts — not the kitchen sink; fundraising is progressive discovery.
4. A team block — "60–70% of the early-stage decision is driven by team quality."
5. Teaser-deck link (≤12 slides, PDF, tracked) + **contact info** ("something founders often forget").

The goal is not "I'll invest" — it's the connector's counterpart saying "this looks interesting, please connect me." Self-test before sending: *"If I received this blurb, would I feel compelled to pass it along?"* If no, rewrite (Metal). The referrer-facing note itself stays <100 words, forwardable without editing, subject like "*[Referrer], can you introduce me to [Partner]?*" plus the single most exciting fact.

**Referrer selection and care:**
- Choose on closeness × relevance. Best-in-class: **a well-performing portfolio founder of that exact partner**; a struggling portfolio founder's referral carries negative signal (Techstars × Metal).
- 1–2 great referrers beat 10 average ones — investors weight referrers (NFX).
- Intro power ranking (NFX, 2025): a respected ecosystem name or a founder they've backed (especially one investing) → another VC they trust who already invested → a short, excellently crafted cold email → worst: bankers, "fundraising agents," or anyone taking a percentage for intros — that channel marks the deal.
- **Pump up the referrers** — they're going out on a limb; the difference between "I can't really vouch" and "you *have* to speak to Chris" (Techstars × Metal).
- Etiquette note (authored, corpus-thin): offer the connector a double-opt-in path — let them check the investor's interest before connecting; the corpus's only trace is a VC noting "if I make investor intros, I actually ask for permission."

**Network-mining tactics that actually execute:**
- **Advisor columns:** add a named column per advisor to the shared CRM, invite them in, ask them to populate from their rolodex — ~60 warm intros from 5–6 advisors (Foundersuite).
- **Power-connector batch email:** when one person is the mutual across many targets, send one email listing all the names — they reply yes/no down the list; then one forwardable thread per yes.
- **The founder back-channel:** no path to a partner? Cold-email 2–3 founders that partner backed in the last 6–12 months, ask what the partner is like to work with, then ask for the intro — "there's a great pay-it-forward ethos amongst founders."
- **Angel groups:** never pitch the group cold — convert one member with genuine affinity into a champion first (Raiz, 2023). Also: university alumni investing groups and company alumni funds (Uber/PayPal mafias).
- **Partner vs associate:** the Partner makes the call — go direct when possible (Antler); associates are the *pre-raise* entry with the highest read rate (their job is sourcing — SVB). Full associate protocol in `meetings.md`.

## Cold-email spec

Objective: a reply, from an investor who reads and decides in 60–120 seconds (Seibel).

- **Subject <60 chars / 6–10 words**: thesis-fit signal + excellence signal + stage (+ company). Formula: `B2B SaaS, $800K ARR, raising $2M seed`. Never "investment opportunity."
- **Line 1 is the why-you** — a named portfolio company, thesis post, or recent action; write it before anything else.
- **Body <1,000 characters / <200 words** (pure cold: <100 words converts best). 3–5 sentences + 2–3 bullets. Plain text — "a sane person doesn't write a thousand words in a first email" (Calacanis).
- Say what the company does in plain words; facts first, origin story never; at least one hard number ("MRR grew 25% MoM for 12 months", never "traction is encouraging"; and 1→20 users is not "20× growth").
- **Deck linked in the first email** (never "may I send the deck?"): ≤12 slides, PDF, clean filename (`Tesla - Seed round`, not `V6 - Pitch deck FIN`), tracked host. Exactly one link, zero images (spam filters).
- CEO sends, from a company address containing their name. Signature with mobile. One specific front-loaded ask — a 20-minute call ("as requested meeting length goes up, probability goes down" — Barsh; angels: 15 minutes, "anytime, anywhere" — Calacanis). Never "pick your brain."
- Break the ice first: LinkedIn connect with a note, follow on X — several notifications land before the email (Barsh).
- Send weekday working hours **in the investor's timezone**, Tue–Thu mornings best; angels also respond evenings/weekends.
- Working exemplar pattern (John Hu/Stan's 25-billionaire campaign): subject = **shared-identity bridge** ("3+ year Stripe customer reaching out", "fellow UNC alum reaching out"), body = specific credible why-them + one hard number + one concrete ask. Outcome: mostly rejections, then one yes weeks later after follow-ups — "you just need one to land."

## Follow-up cadence and stop rules

- Key on deck-tracking state (OpenVC): **opened + no reply → it's a no** — one courtesy nudge, move on ("not replying is protocol for VCs; that's their way of declining"). **Not opened → follow up once, then twice, 3–7 days apart**, then pass.
- First follow-up 5–7 business days out, leading with **one fresh data point**; most replies land on touch 2–3 (Qubit).
- Re-engagement later in the process: milestone-anchored, ≤150 words, exactly one question — never "just checking in."
- Post-meeting follow-ups are `meetings.md` territory (≤24h, mirror their phrasing).

## Sequencing and clustering

- Practice ladder: less-relevant investors → relevant lower-tier → Tier A once the pitch is fluent and signals exist (NFX; Antler's 2–3 rehearsal meetings).
- **Blitzkrieg:** fire all intro requests in the same window — intros have days-to-weeks latency, and batching the *requests* is what clusters the meetings (Foundersuite).
- Compress first meetings into a 2–3-week window, max 4–5 pitches/day; 50–60 funds ≈ 3 weeks (Lenny's). First wave of ~10 slots, release tranches as no's come in — "more isn't necessarily better" (Antler). Batching manufactures competition: "nothing speeds them up more than feeling like they're competing" (Copybara).

## Pre-raise nurture (starts ~6 months out)

- **Permission email** (SVB, aimed at associates): who you are, one-line company, why-them, age + raised + most impressive result, next-quarter goal — single ask: "may I add you to our monthly update list?" Most say yes; sourcing is their job.
- Monthly update to the pipeline: recurring subject `[company] [month, year] update`, BCC'd, with metrics, next focus, and a challenges section (a hook for help). Announce the round 30 days before kickoff — "I've seen investors swoop up a deal before it even goes to market" (Foundersuite).
- Bank 6–8 "tell me when you're out" expressions of interest six months early (Kevin Ryan) — the conversation is safer when nothing is being asked. Boundary (Casado, a16z): "either you are raising or you aren't" — the pre-process is legitimate while the ask is genuinely advice and the raise decision isn't made; soliciting while denying it reads terribly when the round opens weeks later.
- Ask each early conversation for a 1–10 interest rating; at 7+, ask what the steps to invest would be (CRV). Sequoia met Thumbtack 3 years before investing.
- Full update templates and the always-on cadence: `process-and-pipeline.md`.

## Deliverability engineering (volume campaigns only)

SPF + DKIM + DMARC configured; sending addresses warmed ≥2 weeks and kept warming; list verified, bounce rate <1%; ~20 sends/mailbox/day, 2 mailboxes/domain, secondary domains (`company.co`), inboxes split across Google and Office365; budget 10–15 min of personalization per email — 100 VCs ≈ 16 hours (OpenVC, 2026).
