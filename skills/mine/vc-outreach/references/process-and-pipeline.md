# Process & Pipeline — momentum, telemetry, updates

The full readiness and cadence checklist applies to an active raise. A message, list, or meeting task needs only its relevant facts and next step; no CRM/data-room/bootstrap prerequisite.

The raise is a designed process: "fundraising is driven by momentum… it's very hard to get momentum going if you're running a sloppy fundraise" (Foundersuite). Timeline reality: 6–10 weeks first meeting → term sheet, 12–16 weeks → wired (2026 seed); median seed close stretched 68 → 142 days from 2021 to 2025 — and that median already sits past CRV's "raising 4+ months signals issues" line, which is exactly why the process below is engineered rather than drifted. Seasons are real (NFX, 2025): January→spring break and Labor Day→Thanksgiving are the hot windows; summer is the hidden season — fewer competitors, vacation-broken momentum (Casado calls the summer rule an anachronism; hedge by how many partners you need in one room); holidays are dead. Plan ~12 months before cash-out, prep seriously at 9, and **fundraise inside one season** — a raise spanning two reads as a market verdict: skip a season, fix metrics and story, return ("investors remember who was out in spring when they see you again in the fall").

## Pre-kickoff readiness (all checked before the first intro request)

- [ ] Target list built, filtered ~30%, tiered A/B/C, referrer-mapped (`targeting.md`).
- [ ] Data room assembled **now, not after a term sheet** (`diligence-and-close.md`); running FAQ doc started.
- [ ] Deck: clean outreach version (no customer names) + detailed appendix; final PDF locked, version-controlled — no `Feb Deck FIN v12` sent in April.
- [ ] The forecast investors see = the forecast the board sees. No fundraise-only model.
- [ ] Customer references identified and warmed ~3 months out; testimonial videos pre-recorded to ration live calls.
- [ ] Downside scenarios pre-planned (minimum viable capital, path to profitability) before emotions are in play.
- [ ] Calendar: 2–3-week pitch window blocked; 2 full days/week (or 4 h/day) reserved for the raise.
- [ ] CRM live with the eight stages; every row has an owner and a dated next step; shared read-only with advisors/board for gap-flagging.
- [ ] Momentum answer scripted (below).

## Pipeline stages and telemetry

Stages (Metal, 2026): **Researching → Outreach Sent → First Meeting Scheduled → Meeting Completed → Follow-Up Sent → Due Diligence → Soft Circled → Passed.** "Soft Circled" = committed *with conditions* — a distinct stage whose work is clearing those conditions. "Passed" is recoverable: early passes reconsider on material progress or a named lead.

Health benchmarks (Techstars × Metal, 2025):
- **Intro opt-in rate** (investor says yes to the forwarded blurb): 25–50% healthy; ~20% acceptable; **1-in-20 = the blurb/positioning is broken**.
- **First call → second call ≥ 1 in 10**; below = targeting or positioning is wrong.
- **Velocity through stages is the single biggest predictor of funding success** — above traction, pedigree, or network.
- Full-process shape to expect: 30 well-screened partners → 20 first calls → 10 engaged → 5 dig in → 1–2 term sheets (a sloppy list needs ~60 partners for the same result — Pillar).

## Parallelism done right

- **Blitzkrieg the intro requests** (the upstream lever): all connector asks fired in the same window so meetings cluster.
- **Same-pace rule** (Northflank): parallel *stage*, not just parallel calendar — "you want investors entering their second and third meetings simultaneously." The named failure: deep in partner meetings with a lukewarm fund while the favourite is at meeting one. Weekly, slow the leader or accelerate the laggard.
- Meetings remote-first; in-person reserved for real interest.
- Compressed window: 2–3 weeks of first meetings "creates the conditions for desire and scarcity" (Rohan/Altman via Lenny's). Pre-emptive term sheet outside the window → gracious power flip: "that's when we're accepting term sheets" — "this will not turn them off; likely the opposite."
- Caution (Lemkin): a forced 3-day decision window from a merely-interesting company produces instant passes — compression works only with real heat.

## Ethical momentum (what works vs what backfires)

Works: factual, name-free momentum ("third meeting with a top multi-stage fund tomorrow") · a stated close date actually intended, restated every meeting · opening a SAFE for small angel checks early as social proof · after a term sheet exists, telling others transparently and asking what's left in their process.

Backfires: naming the term-sheet giver ("mystery is always more seductive than the truth — describe them accurately in the abstract, and never lie") · an angel pressuring the lead ("savvy VCs take this pressure as a sign of weakness"; the only backchannel that works is an existing investor *vouching*) · claiming term sheets or interest that doesn't exist ("everyone talks; it's a fast way to kill a process" — and 7 of 10 lies surface in diligence).

The momentum answer, scripted and honest, for "how's the round coming together?": early — "started last week; 10 meetings this week, 12 next, closing by March 1st; running a tight process." Later — "in diligence with four firms; expecting two term sheets by Friday."

Deadline design (Kevin Ryan, who raised $750M): a hard, dated term-sheet deadline 3–4 weeks after the final first meetings, then ~one week to decide — not six hours, not four months. Make every firm feel like the favourite ("how can you convince every VC — every one of them — that they're your favorite?"); signal competition without naming names, and never quote a big count of firms in play — "37 VCs" reads as a lie.

## The weekly review (30–45 min, same slot; numeric triggers)

1. Stage sort: anything in Contacted **>7 days** → follow-up scheduled now; Meeting Scheduled → deck send + calendar confirm queued.
2. Every row: dated next step with an owner, or demote.
3. Engagement sort (deck opens / data-room views): high engagement + no recent touch → personal email; **zero engagement 30 days → final nudge or Passed** (kept for next round).
4. Same-pace check across tiers.
5. Counts: new leads, new soft circles, $ soft-circled vs target.
6. Objections-ledger review (`meetings.md`).
7. NFX reflection: what did I learn; what might I be misreading?

"This is the workflow that separates founders who close in 8 weeks from founders who drag for 6 months" (Visible, 2026). A spreadsheet stops working at ~20 live relationships; the average active pipeline runs ~52 investors.

## Investor updates — two tracks

**Track 1, prospective (pipeline tool, starts pre-raise):** permission email → monthly milestone update, BCC'd, recurring subject `[company] [month, year] update`: what we do (one line — they see hundreds) → last month's numbers → product shot → next month → team photo → challenges (the help hook). Planning-then-reporting proves three attributes at once: plan, execute, report. No burn/cash disclosure to prospects. The day after closing, start the next round's list and keep this cadence running — plus Lemkin's 1-and-30: one VC meeting a week, permanently ("every VC would love to watch your progress for 2–3 months before they invest"). This is a live disagreement with PG's binary rule — in fundraising mode or fully out, refusing any meeting that needs convincing. PG optimizes founder focus and compression; Lemkin/Ryan optimize pre-existing conviction. Pick one deliberately; a workable line is updates yes, pitches no.

**Track 2, existing investors (obligation, post-close):** monthly, never skipped, bad news never sat on — "radio silence gives investors time to ruminate… we will assume the worst" (NFX). Template (NFX, 2023): executive summary in the body *and* attachment — Highlights (3–5 bullets) · Lowlights · KPIs (only the ones that matter) · **Requests (max 4, bold the top one)** · Runway with and without hitting forecast. Main doc: KPI deltas since last update · achievements (≤2 bullets per domain) · challenges including small ones ("easy assists") · plans + target KPIs for next update. Off-cycle triggers: layoffs, a major pitch coming, big news. Board meetings (6–12-week cadence) are too slow to substitute — updates are "chapters in a story," and the raise-time payoff is investors who watched the story compound.
